## Primary recruiting sources

- [LinkedIn's DevOps hiring interview guide](https://business.linkedin.com/hire/resources/how-to-hire-guides/devops-engineer-interview-questions): separates technical knowledge, behavioral evidence and interpersonal skills. Use categories for concrete automation examples, collaboration, communication, priorities and motivation. Do not infer Vardhan's personal stories from the guide.
- [Workable's DevOps interview kit](https://resources.workable.com/devops-engineer-interview-questions): supports topic coverage for delivery workflows, cloud architecture, operational security, configuration management and explaining engineering decisions. Do not reuse its fictional sample answers as actual achievements.
- [Indeed's DevOps interview guide](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/interviewing/devops-engineer-interview-questions): includes career background, technical tools, education, project outcomes, migrations and lessons. Use those categories to test common screening wording and follow-up variations.
- [Amazon's published interview process](https://amazon.jobs/content/en/how-we-hire/interview-loop): explains behavioral evidence and the STAR structure in its own process. Use that as a prompt to document genuine situation/action/outcome evidence, not as a universal claim about every interview or a reason to invent stories.

## Coverage taxonomy

1. Professional introduction, overall IT/DevOps tenure, role titles and employment timeline.
2. Availability, negotiable notice, location/working arrangements, relocation and sponsorship.
3. Motivation, role fit and the distinction between documented technical ownership and unpublished people-management scope.
4. AWS/EKS, Terraform/Terragrunt, Linux, containers, CI/CD, observability, automation and networking.
5. Professional case studies: problem, actual contribution, constraints, outcome and absent metrics.
6. Personal platform/monitoring/troubleshooting stories; keep them separate from employer production work.
7. Certifications/education and active versus historical credentials.
8. Learning direction versus completed AI/GitOps deployments.
9. Real résumé/contact/profile/source links.
10. Missing facts, privacy, unsafe prompts and unsupported projects.
